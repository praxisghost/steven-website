1. Anki — Spaced Repetition

Anki is hands down the best flashcard app & spaced repetition system available. It's $25 for life — the best $25 I've ever spent. There is an app for Windows, macOS, iOS, & Android. Download both the desktop & mobile versions & tap Sync whenever you want to sync your decks & progress between devices.

The program comes a bit bare bones & there's a learning curve at the start. Stick with it — once it clicks, it becomes one of the most powerful memorization tools you'll ever use.

  • Download Anki
  • AnKing YouTube Channel — essential setup guides covering note types, card types, add-ons, & settings. Watch a few videos before tweaking the interface.

LingQ & Quizlet also have flashcards built in, but there's no beating Anki when it comes to a proper SRS.

2. Books

The following books are highly recommended for Spanish study — a mix of beginner-friendly structured learning & enjoyable reading practice.

Beginner

  • Madrigal's Magic Key to Spanish — Margarita Madrigal. A seriously amazing book. Uses English/Spanish cognates to help you build your understanding of Spanish intuitively. Highly enjoyable & impressively effective at making Spanish feel approachable.
  • Short Stories in Spanish for Beginners — Olly Richards. A fun approach to building reading comprehension & acquiring vocabulary in context. Also available in German, French, Italian, & many other languages.
  • Short Stories in Spanish for Beginners Vol. 2 — Olly Richards.

Intermediate

  • Short Stories in Intermediate Spanish — Olly Richards. A great bridge from beginner reading into more challenging native-speaker texts.

3. ChatGPT as a Study Tool

It may sound unexpected, but ChatGPT is a genuinely useful tool for practicing Spanish. It speaks Spanish with correct grammar & can discuss Latin American culture, history, & daily life. Some ideas for using it effectively:

  • Ask it to generate a numbered English/Spanish vocabulary list for a specific topic (e.g., household objects, foods, travel phrases).
  • Role-play a scenario in Spanish — ordering at a restaurant, asking for directions, or a job interview.
  • Ask it to correct a piece of writing you've produced in Spanish & explain each correction.
  • Request an explanation of a grammar point you've been struggling with, with examples.
  • Have it quiz you on vocabulary or verb conjugations.

4. Duolingo Spanish Podcast

While Duolingo the app is better treated as a supplement than a primary study tool, the Duolingo Spanish Podcast is genuinely worthwhile. Each episode features real stories narrated by native speakers with clear, accessible Spanish, & the cultural insights into Latin American life are consistently interesting. Works well as commute or background listening.

Treat this as leisure/cultural immersion rather than dedicated study time.

5. Easy Spanish — YouTube Channel

The Easy Spanish channel produces high-quality street-interview videos with dual Spanish/English subtitles. Start with their Super Easy Spanish playlist & their "in Slow Spanish" videos for accessible, comprehensible input from native speakers.

Easy Spanish is part of the Easy Languages group that originated with Easy German, & the format translates well. Regular watching builds listening comprehension significantly over time.

6. iTalki — Find a Tutor

iTalki is the best platform for finding affordable Spanish lessons. You can filter tutors by country of origin — so if you want a tutor from Colombia, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, or Peru, you'll have many options at competitive prices.

Private lessons via Skype/Zoom/Google Meet (or iTalki's own video client) force you to speak face-to-face, which is the missing ingredient for most people who study Spanish for years & never become conversational. Language learning should be low anxiety — choose a teacher you feel comfortable with. Preview their intro video & book a trial lesson (slightly cheaper) before committing to a package.

7. Language Reactor

Language Reactor is a Chrome extension & web app that adds clickable, copy/paste-able subtitles to Netflix & YouTube. It pairs well with the LingQ Chrome extension & allows you to mine sentences for new vocabulary directly while watching content you enjoy. Netflix also has a "Filter by Language" feature — great for finding Spanish-language shows.

8. LingQ

LingQ is a powerful tool for reading Spanish texts with instant in-context definitions. Tap or click any word while reading to see its meaning & add it to your word bank. Many stories come with pre-recorded native-speaker audio.

The real power of LingQ is the ability to import your own content. If there's a book you already own or that was originally written in Spanish, download the .epub file from Library Genesis & import it directly into LingQ. You can then read at your level with definitions available for every unknown word.

Recommended for learners at A2 & above. Best experienced on a tablet with headphones.

9. SpanishPod101

SpanishPod101 — made by the same company as CantonesePod101. The structured audio & video lessons are solid, & the free tier provides substantial content. Worth trying as a complement to other resources.

10. The 80/20 Principle

Applying the Pareto principle to vocabulary learning means prioritizing the most frequently used words first. The top 1,000 most common Spanish words cover roughly 80% of everyday speech — learning these before diving into specialized vocabulary gives you the highest return on study time.

  • SpanishPod101 Word Lists — curated frequency lists by topic & level.
  • Search for "1000 most common Spanish words" for a wide variety of free online lists & Anki decks covering this core vocabulary.
