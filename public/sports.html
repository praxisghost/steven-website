Sports

Activities I play, have played, or want to play more of.

Bowling

Bowling is one of those sports that rewards consistency more than raw athleticism, which I find appealing. There's real technique in it — release, spin, lane reading — and the social setting makes it easy to keep playing without it feeling like a grind. I enjoy both casual and more competitive frames.

Ultimate Frisbee

Ultimate is one of my favorite team sports. The combination of athleticism, strategy, and the self-refereed spirit of the game makes it unlike most other sports I've played. The cutting, throwing, and field vision required make it technically interesting, and the culture around Ultimate tends to attract people who play hard but stay good-natured about it.

Soccer

Soccer is the most universal sport in the world and it's hard not to love it. I enjoy playing recreationally — the flow of the game, the spacing, the constant movement. I'm also a fan of watching it at the club and international level; there's a tactical depth to soccer that becomes more visible the more you play.

Kayaking

Kayaking appeals to me because of where it takes you — rivers, coastlines, lakes you wouldn't otherwise reach. It's a sport that rewards patience and technique over strength, and it connects directly to my interest in aquatic environments. Flatwater kayaking is meditative; moving water is something else entirely.

Badminton

Badminton is faster than most people expect and more technical than it looks. The smash, the drop shot, the net play — each requires a different kind of precision. I enjoy it both as a quick casual game and as a sport worth playing seriously. It's also one of the most accessible racket sports to pick up at a basic level.

Baseball

Baseball is a sport I grew up watching and playing. There's a rhythm to it that I find genuinely enjoyable — the pace, the strategy, the individual confrontations within a team context. Pitch selection, fielding positioning, base running decisions: the game has a tactical layer that rewards attention.

Racquetball

Racquetball is intense in a way few sports are — the enclosed court, the speed of the ball, the constant need to read angles and anticipate. It's one of the best cardiovascular workouts I know of, and the skill ceiling is surprisingly high once you get past the beginner stage and start working on shot placement and serve strategy.

Rock Climbing

Rock climbing is as much a puzzle as it is a physical sport. Reading a route, planning your moves, finding the balance between grip and footwork — it demands a kind of focused problem-solving that I find very satisfying. I've done mostly indoor bouldering and top-rope, but outdoor climbing is something I want to pursue more seriously.

Handball

Handball (the court sport — not the wall game) is widely played in Europe and remains underappreciated in North America. It combines the athleticism of basketball with the physicality of water polo, played at a pace that rarely lets up. I was introduced to it during my time in Germany and found it immediately compelling.

Skiing

Skiing is one of those activities where progress is genuinely satisfying — moving from the bunny slope to blue runs to blacks involves real skill development, not just repetition. I enjoy it both for the physical challenge and for what it puts you in: mountains, open terrain, cold air. There's not much that compares to a clear day on a good run.

Snowboarding

Snowboarding has a different feel from skiing — more fluid, more of a surfing or skateboarding sensibility transferred to snow. I've done both and find them different enough to be worth pursuing separately. Snowboarding in particular rewards commitment; hesitating tends to end badly.

Skateboarding

Skateboarding is a sport I've had an on-and-off relationship with. It's unforgiving to learn — the falls are real and the skill curve is steep — but the creativity it allows is unlike any other sport. You're not just performing within a set framework; you're deciding what's even worth attempting. That freedom is something I find hard to replicate elsewhere.
