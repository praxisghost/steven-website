1. About Turkish

Turkish is an agglutinative language of the Turkic family, spoken by roughly 85 million people primarily in Turkey and Cyprus. It is entirely unrelated to English, Arabic, or the major European languages — but it is internally very regular. Once the core grammar rules are learned, they apply consistently with few exceptions, which makes Turkish rewarding for systematic learners.

Key features to be aware of from day one

  • Vowel harmony. Turkish vowels form two groups (front: e, i, ö, ü / back: a, ı, o, u). Suffixes change their vowel to harmonize with the last vowel of the root. This governs a large proportion of Turkish grammar and must be internalized early.
  • Agglutination. Turkish builds meaning by chaining suffixes onto a root. A single Turkish word can express what English requires an entire phrase for (e.g., gelemiyor musunuz? — "are you not able to come?").
  • SOV word order. The verb comes at the end of the sentence. The subject and object can be moved for emphasis, but the verb is almost always last.
  • No grammatical gender. Turkish has no masculine/feminine distinction — the same pronoun o covers he, she, and it.
  • Postpositions, not prepositions. Where English says "for you", Turkish says the equivalent of "you for" — the relational word follows the noun it modifies.

3. Quick Language Notes

The Turkish Alphabet

Turkish uses a Latin-based alphabet of 29 letters introduced in 1928. Most letters are pronounced as in English, but several require attention:

LetterApproximate soundExample
ç"ch" as in churchçok (very)
ş"sh" as in shipşimdi (now)
ğsilent — lengthens the preceding voweldağ (mountain)
ıa central "uh" vowel — no English equivalent; like the schwa in sofakız (girl)
ö"ur" as in nurse (rounded lips)görmek (to see)
ü"u" as in French tu (rounded front vowel)üç (three)

Verb Infinitives

Turkish verb infinitives end in -mak or -mek (the vowel follows harmony). In the flashcard deck, verbs are listed in infinitive form so you see the root clearly. When conjugating, drop the -mak/-mek to get the stem, then add tense and person suffixes.

InfinitiveRootPresent (I)Meaning
gelmekgel-geliyorumI am coming
gitmekgit-gidiyorumI am going
yapmakyap-yapıyorumI am doing
bilmekbil-biliyorumI know

Negation

değil — placed after a noun or adjective to negate it. "Ben Türk değilim" = "I am not Turkish."
For verb negation, the suffix -me/-ma is inserted before the tense suffix: "gelmiyorum" = "I am not coming."

The Buffer var / yok

Turkish uses var ("there is") and yok ("there isn't") as standalone words to express existence and possession — no "to have" verb exists. "Param var" (my money there-is) = "I have money." "Param yok" = "I don't have money."
