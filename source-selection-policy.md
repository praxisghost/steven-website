# Source Selection Policy

## Preferred Sources

Always attempt to use:

1. .gov
2. .org

Examples:

* Government agencies
* National archives
* Museums
* Language preservation organizations
* Educational nonprofits
* International organizations
* Research institutes

## Alternative Sources

Only if no suitable .gov or .org source exists.

These sources must pass a substantially higher quality threshold.

Examples:

* Universities
* Peer-reviewed journals
* Academic publishers
* Major linguistic organizations
* National libraries

Possible domains:

* .edu
* .ac.uk
* .de
* .ch
* .jp
* .fr
* .ca
* .net
* .com

## Rejection Criteria

Never use:

* Blogs
* Personal websites
* AI-generated content
* Content farms
* SEO sites
* Unsourced educational sites
* Forum posts
* Social media
* Marketing content

## Citation Eligibility Test

Before adding a source, answer:

1. Is the claim important?
2. Does the source directly support the claim?
3. Is the source highly reliable?
4. Does the citation improve the page?

If all four answers are not YES:

Do not cite.

## Important

Do not add citations simply to justify an opinion.

Do not add citations simply because a source exists.

Only cite where a reader would reasonably benefit from supporting evidence.

## Typical Claims Worth Citing

* Historical claims
* Population statistics
* Speaker counts
* Endangerment status
* Government policies
* Academic findings
* Linguistic classifications
* Historical events
* Demographic data
* Scientific conclusions

## Typical Claims Not Worth Citing

* Common knowledge
* Editorial opinions
* Learning advice
* General introductions
* Grammar explanations
* Transition paragraphs
* Personal observations
